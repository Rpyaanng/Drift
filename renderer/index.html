Help: friends joining from anywhere

Do you need this?

Probably not. Friends on your own Wi-Fi can always join, and most routers let Drift open the way for friends elsewhere by itself. This page is only for when Drift tells you that friends elsewhere might not get in.

Nothing here can break your internet. Every step can be undone by deleting what you added, and Drift only lets in someone who has your invite or share code.

The easy way: turn on UPnP

UPnP lets apps like Drift (and most games and consoles) ask the router to open a port while they need it. Drift closes it again when you stop. If your router has UPnP switched off, switching it on is all you need: look for UPnP in the router's settings (steps 2 and 3 below show how to get there), turn it on, then share or host again.

Or forward the ports yourself

  1. Your PC's addressYour router needs to know which PC the friends are coming to. This PC is …
  2. Open your router's settingsType 192.168.1.1 into any web browser's address bar. If nothing comes up, try 192.168.0.1 or 10.0.0.1. The sign-in name and password are often on a sticker on the router. Some routers, like Google Nest Wifi, eero, or your internet provider's own, use a phone app instead.
  3. Find port forwardingIt's often under Advanced, NAT or Firewall, and may be called Port forwarding, Virtual servers, Port mapping or Applications & gaming.
  4. Add these rulesPoint each one at …. If the router asks for a start and an end port, put the same number in both. Name them anything, like "Drift".
    ForPortProtocol
    Watching together47800TCP
    Your hub's chat47900TCP
    Voice calls in your hub47900UDP
  5. Save, and try itShare or host your hub again. When Drift can tell the way is open, the warning goes away. Some routers can't check this from the inside, so the best test is asking a friend to join.

If it still doesn't work

Your PC's address changed. Routers sometimes give a PC a new address after a restart. Look for DHCP reservation or Static IP in the router and reserve … for this PC.

Your internet provider shares one address between homes. Some do (it's called CGNAT, and it's common on mobile and satellite internet). Then no router setting can open the way. Ask your provider for a public IP address, or let a friend whose connection works host the hub instead.

Changed your mind? Delete the rules you added. Drift goes on working for friends on your own Wi-Fi.
